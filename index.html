Pause Before the Next Patient

A brief post-call wellbeing and reflection tool for ambulance clinicians.

Following a challenging or emotionally significant job, clinicians may need to transition rapidly to their next patient.

This prototype provides an optional 1–2 minute opportunity to:

  • Check in with yourself
  • Reset after a difficult call
  • Briefly reflect
  • Identify whether you need support
  • Prepare yourself for the next patient
Prototype only. This is not a clinical assessment and does not replace local Trust support, clinical supervision or emergency procedures.

You have just completed a patient encounter.

If you feel that the job has affected you, take 1–2 minutes before moving on.

Scan to access the wellbeing resource

For demonstration only. A real deployment would use an approved Trust-hosted link.

1. Check in

How are you feeling following that job?

What are you noticing?

Nothing here needs to be recorded or transmitted. It is simply a moment to notice how you are feeling.

2. Reset

Take 60–90 seconds before moving on.

STOP

Give yourself a moment.

TAKE A BREATH

Take three slow breaths. Allow your shoulders to relax.

OBSERVE

What am I feeling? Where am I holding tension?

PREPARE

Ground yourself. Unclench your jaw. Relax your hands. Take a drink if appropriate.

You have just finished one patient's story.
The next patient deserves your attention too.

3. Reflect

A quick reflection — you don't need to write an essay.

What went well?

What did I find difficult?

Is there anything I would do differently?

Is there anything I need to discuss?

What do I need to take forward — and what can I leave with this job?

Prototype: entries are not saved or transmitted anywhere.

4. Prepare

What do I need before the next patient?

There is no “wrong” answer. Recognising that you need something is part of looking after yourself and maintaining safe practice.

5. Am I ready for the next patient?

The aim is awareness, not a pass/fail test.

If you need support

Your crewmate

Sometimes simply talking through what happened can help.

Your colleagues / team leader

Speak to someone you trust or use the appropriate operational or clinical support pathway.

Your Trust's wellbeing services

Access the relevant wellbeing, occupational health or employee support service.

Further professional support

Use the appropriate service available within your organisation.

If you don't feel able to continue safely: tell someone.

This resource is not a substitute for clinical, occupational or psychological support.

Remember

Looking after yourself does not mean you are not resilient. Recognising when a job has affected you is part of professional self-awareness.
